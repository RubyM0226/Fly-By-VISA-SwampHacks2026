### What information can I obtain from the I-94/I-95 website?

The I-94/I-95 website consists of four main sections:

1.  **Apply for a Provisional Form I-94:** Enables users to apply for a Provisional I-94 up to seven days before arriving in the United States at a port of entry or select ferry locations. For more details, refer to the ["Provisional I-94s"](https://i94.cbp.dhs.gov/help#Need-to-Learn-How-to-Apply-for-an-I-94-Web) section of the FAQs.

2.  **Get Most Recent I-94/I-95:** Allows retrieval and printing of most recent I-94 admission record for most classes of admission or parole.

3.  **View Travel History:** Provides access to the past 10 years of travel history for certain foreign nationals.

4.  **View Compliance:** Displays the remaining authorized time in the United States and overstay status for specific classes of admission, including:

    -   Visa Waiver Program (VWP)
    -   Guam-Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands Visa Waiver Program (G-CNMI VWP)
    -   CNMI Economic Vitality & Security Travel Authorization Program (EVS-TAP)

### What is the difference between the Form I-95 and I-94?

The Form I-95 is the Crewman’s Landing Permit which is issued only to working alien crewmembers on a vessel or aircraft. The I-95 gives the working alien crewmember permission to enter the United States for up to 29 days or depart with their vessel or aircraft. Whichever comes first.

### How long can I stay in the United States?

The Form I-94 is the Arrival/Departure Form that is issued to only non-immigrant aliens entering the United States for tourism, business, employment, or student status. The I-94 allows the alien non-immigrant to remain in the United States until the Admit Until Date annotated on their Form I-94 by the admitting CBP officer.

### What if someone accessed my I-94 or travel history without my consent?

Any unauthorized access, use, or modification of this system or its data is a violation of 18 U.S. Code § 1030 and other criminal laws, potentially resulting in civil or criminal liability and permanent denial of access to the site. CBP reserves the right to take appropriate action to enforce its rights under the Terms of Use and may work with law enforcement authorities to investigate and prosecute violations.

If you believe your I-94 or travel history has been accessed without your consent, please report the incident by emailing: [privacy@dhs.gov](mailto:privacy@dhs.gov).

### I am a United States Citizen or Lawful Permanent Resident. Can I retrieve my travel history by using the I-94/I-95 website?

No, United States Citizens and Lawful Permanent Residents cannot retrieve their travel history using the I-94/I-95 website. Currently, only nonimmigrant can use this feature. To obtain your travel history records, you will need to submit a request through the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA). Please refer to the ["Freedom of Information Act (FOIA)"](https://i94.cbp.dhs.gov/help#what-is-a-freedom-of-information-act-request) section of the FAQs for instructions on how to submit your request.

### Will CBP provide a traveler with documentation or evidence showing their status and authorized time in the United States?

Yes. Upon arrival at a U.S. port of entry, nonimmigrant travelers are issued an electronic Form I-94. Travelers can access their electronic arrival/departure record through the CBP I-94/I-95 website [(https://I94.cbp.dhs.gov)](https://i94.cbp.dhs.gov/) or by using the free CBP Link Mobile Application on an Android or Apple device. Individuals lawfully admitted or paroled into the United States may print their official Form I-94 from the website or the mobile app. If you are asked to provide proof of your admission, this is the document you should present.

### Does the I-94 automation process help eliminate errors, and what should a traveler do if their Form I-94 or admission is incorrect?

Yes, the electronic I-94 helps reduce errors by using information from official travel document. However, if there is an error on either the paper or electronic Form I-94 issued by CBP, or if a nonimmigrant traveler was admitted incorrectly to the United States, they should contact their nearest Deferred Inspection office within thirty days of admission or parole, or you may submit an inquiry through the CBP Ask a Question portal at [https://www.help.cbp.gov/s/questions?language=en\_US.](https://www.help.cbp.gov/s/questions?language=en_US) A list of Deferred Inspection Sites and Ports of Entry is available on the CBP website [(https://www.cbp.gov)](https://www.cbp.gov/) under the "Ports" link.

If your Form I-94 that was issued by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Service (USCIS), please contact USCIS for assistance with the correction or replacement of your Form I-94 at [www.uscis.gov](https://www.uscis.gov/).

### How can a traveler revalidate their electronic Form I-94?

If your I-94 admission record is electronic, your I-94 is maintained in CBP systems and can be verified by a CBP officer for automatic visa revalidation, provided you meet the eligibility requirements. To demonstrate eligibility when boarding a carrier to the United States, you may need to print a copy of your electronic I-94 from the CBP website and present it to the air or sea carrier before boarding.

If you were admitted to the United States and received a paper Form I-94, you must present the valid, unexpired paper I-94 and required travel documents to meet automatic revalidation requirements.

For more information about automatic revalidation, please visit the U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) Information Center [Automatic revalidation for certain temporary visitors](https://www.help.cbp.gov/s/article/Article-1446?language=en_US).

### How does CBP's automation of Form I-94 affect the I-9 process?

Form I-9, administered by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS), is used to verify the identity and employment authorization of individuals hired in the United States. The automation of Form I-94 does not impact the Form I-9 process. However, the public may notice that I-94 forms now appear differently, some may have original stamps and handwriting, while others may be printouts without any original writing or stamps. Nonimmigrant travelers can print their Form I-94 from the CBP website using the "Get Most Recent I-94/I-95" function or through the CBP Link Mobile Application. Both versions are acceptable for Form I-9 purposes. For more information about Form I-9, visit [https://www.uscis.gov/i-9](https://www.uscis.gov/i-9).

### Where do I go to get additional information about the I-94 automation?

If you have additional questions about the I-94 automation, please visit [https://www.cbp.gov/document/fa-sheets/i-94-fact-sheet](https://www.cbp.gov/document/fact-sheets/i-94-fact-sheet).

### Can a Form I-94 be used for multiple land or sea entries?

A valid, unexpired Form I-94 issued under most classes of admission may permit multiple entries to the United States by land or sea from contiguous territories (Canada, Mexico, or Adjacent Islands (excluding Cuba)). The traveler may remain in the United States until the "Admit Until Date" specified on their I-94.

**Note:** Short trips (less than 30 days) by land or ferry to contiguous territories will not result in a new Form I-94 or extend your current "Admit Until Date."

For assistance with reentry to the United States after visiting a contiguous territory, please contact the CBP Information Center at 1-877-227-5511 (inside the United States) or 00+1+202-325-8000 (outside the United States).

### What does my I-94 number look like?

I-94 numbers issued before May 2019 are 11 digits long and consist only of numbers (e.g., 01234567890).

Starting in May 2019, CBP began issuing alphanumeric I-94 numbers. These numbers are also 11 characters long and follow the format of nine numbers, one letter, and one final number (e.g., 012345678A1). Unexpired I-94s issued in the numeric-only format remain valid until the "Admit Until Date" shown on the paper I-94 or in the "Get Most Recent I-94/I-95" section of the website, unless otherwise terminated.

### How do I print my Form I-94?

Nonimmigrant travelers can print their Form I-94 by using the "Get Most Recent I-94/I-95" option on the CBP website or through the CBP Link Mobile Application. After retrieving your I-94, a "print" button will appear at the bottom of the results page, allowing you to print the information. You may print as many copies as needed; there is no limit.

### How long will my admission record and Form I-94 be available for retrieval on the I-94/I-95 website?

Most travelers can access their most recent admission record (Form I-94) on the I-94/I-95 website if it was issued after 1983. The website will only display the most recently created I-94 that matches the provided name and date of birth. For example, if a traveler entered the United States in 2005 and received a Form I-94, then returned in 2017 and was issued a new Form I-94, only the 2017 record will be shown. Earlier I-94 records cannot be retrieved.

Travelers admitted for the duration of status (D/S) based on their class of admission can access their Form I-94 information regardless of the creation date, as long as an electronic record exists. Examples of D/S classes of admission include A1, A2, C2, F1, F2, FSM, G1, G2, G3, G4, I, J1, J2, MIS, N1, N2, N3, N4,N5, N6, PAL, and T6.

**Note:** The availability of your I-94 record does not grant legal authority to remain in the United States beyond the terms and conditions of your admission. All travelers must comply with the requirements of their visa classification and the admit until date listed on their I-94.

### Where does the travel history on this site come from?

CBP collects travel information from various sources. The travel history available on this website is intended as a helpful tool for the public and is not an official record for legal purposes. Certain types of travel may not appear on the website, including most land border arrivals and departures, closed loop cruise arrivals and departures, air carrier reservation updates, and I-94s issued by USCIS for changes of status, extensions of stay, or adjustments of status.

Travel history for U.S. citizens and lawful permanent residents is not available on this website. To obtain your travel history, please submit a Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) request at [www.cbp.gov/FOIA](https://www.cbp.gov/site-policy-notices/foia).

### What travel history information can eligible travelers access through the I-94/I-95 website?

CBP maintains records of your United States arrivals and departures. The “View Travel History” section of the I-94/I-95 website will provide your arrival and departure dates; to include the port of entry/exit for the past ten years only.

The travel history provided may not reflect the following:

-   Land departures into Mexico;
-   Air carrier reservation updates; and/or
-   United States Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) updates, changes of status, extensions of stay or adjustments of status.

U.S. Citizens and Lawful Permanent Residents travel history is NOT provided on this website.

### Can I obtain my travel records for a previous or expired passport?

Yes. The website will provide travel history for the past ten years, including any travel conducted on an expired passport. It will also display the most recent I-94 information issued since 1983 for most classes of admission or parole, and indefinitely for certain classes such as diplomats and individuals admitted under the Compacts of Free Association, even if linked to an expired passport. For the "Get Most Recent I-94/I-95" query, you must use the passport number associated with the I-94, regardless of whether the passport has expired.

### What do I need to do if my travel history is incorrect or inaccurate?

There are two processes available to request a review or correction of your travel history. Please select only one process when submitting your request, as submitting both may delay results.

1.  You may request a correction by filing a redress with the CBP Information Center at: [Ask Us a Question](https://www.help.cbp.gov/s/questions?language=en_US). Please choose the appropriate “Topic”: I-94/Traveler Compliance and “Applicable Issue”: Issue with my I-94/Travel History; or
2.  You may file a redress with The Traveler Redress Inquiry Program (DHS TRIP) at: [Traveler Redress Inquiry Program (DHS TRIP) | Homeland Security](https://www.dhs.gov/dhs-trip). Whichever process you choose, you must include supporting evidence showing your travel history is incorrect or inaccurate before a review can be conducted. Processing will be in the order of receipt.

### What is View Compliance function?

The I-94/I-95 website and CBP Link Mobile Application allow Visa Waiver Program (VWP) and Guam Visa Waiver (GWT) travelers to use the "View Compliance" function to review their current admission status in the United States.

This function displays the number of days remaining on their admission or indicates how many days they have overstayed.

Currently, the "View Compliance" function is only available to VWP and GWT travelers.

### What is the source of information used in the View Compliance Check?

The “View Compliance” dates are calculated based on the admit-until date provided on your I-94 by the admitting CBP officer and the date you retrieve your compliance check on this website.

The status indicator may include updates to your immigration status while in the United States; however, any extensions changes to your status through the USCIS while in the United States, may not be reflected.

### What steps should I do if I receive the “10 Days Remaining” reminder email from CBP?

If you received an email indicating that you have 10 days left on your admission and you have already departed, please go to “View Travel History” to confirm your departure is appearing. If you see your departure listed, there is no action to take. The email is automatically generated based on the admission date you received upon entry and serves as a reminder about the time you have left in the United States. The email may have been generated at or near the time you departed the United States.

If your departure is not listed on your travel history, then your departure may not have been properly registered. Some of the most common reasons (not all inclusive) why this may happen is due to:

-   You used a different citizenship (passport) for departure than you used for arrival.
-   You used a slightly different variation of your name on departure than you used on arrival.
-   Your airline misspelled your name or did not list you on the manifest.
-   You departed by land into Mexico.
-   You departed on a transatlantic or open loop cruise, and your cruise line mistakenly indicated your cruise was a closed loop cruise.

To request assistance; please file a redress with the CBP Information Center at: [Ask a Question](https://www.help.cbp.gov/s/questions?language=en_US). Please choose the appropriate “Topic”: I-94/Traveler Compliance and “Applicable Issue”: 10-Day Email Notification. You must submit supporting evidence that you departed timely in order to review your request for correction.

If you are still in the United States and have not departed, you must leave by 11:59 PM Eastern Time (ET) on the last day of your admission, as indicated by the admit until date on your I-94.

### If I have received a 10-Day Email Notification and/or the Traveler Compliance Check shows that I have overstayed my admission; is this a legally binding determination?

**No.** The Traveler Compliance Check is a tool to assist travelers in determining if they have violated their terms of admission. It is not a legally binding determination of your inadmissibility.

A CBP officer will make the determination on your admissibility at the time you present yourself at a port of entry and ask for admission to the United States. Likewise, the U.S. Department of State has the final determination on whether to issue you a new or renewal nonimmigrant visa.

### When I query my name in the View Compliance tab, what does days remaining in the United States mean?

The days remaining in the United Sates are the days you have left on your period of admission as listed on your current Form I-94 under “Admit Until Date”.

### What do I do if I am unable to leave the United States by the last day of my admission?

If you were admitted to the United States under the Visa Waiver Program (VWP), you cannot extend your stay. In very limited circumstances, a local USCIS director or a CBP officer at your nearest Deferred Inspection site may grant a 30-day “Satisfactory Departure” period on a case-by-case basis if you entered as a Visa Waiver traveler. You must demonstrate that extreme or emergency circumstances are preventing your departure from the United States. For more information, refer to USCIS Chapter 2 – Emergencies or Unforeseen Circumstances-Related Flexibilities, or visit a CBP Deferred Inspection Site at [https://www.cbp.gov/contact/ports/deferred-inspection-sites](https://www.cbp.gov/about/contact/ports/deferred-inspection-sites).

If your current class of admission allows extensions, changes, or adjustments and you wish to file a petition to remain past your admit until date on your Form I-94, please contact U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Service (USCIS) for additional assistance or visit USCIS at: [www.uscis.gov](https://www.uscis.gov/).

### What happens if I overstay my admission period?

If you entered the United States under the Visa Waiver Program and stayed longer than the admit until date on your Form I-94, you are no longer eligible for the Electronic System Travel Authorization (ESTA) under Visa Waiver Program and if your ESTA is still valid, it will be denied. You will require a nonimmigrant visa for all future travels to the United States. For information about applying for a U.S. nonimmigrant visa, please visit the U.S. Department of State by visiting [https://www.state.gov](https://www.state.gov/).

If you entered the United States on a nonimmigrant visa, such as a tourist visa (B1/B2) and you stayed longer than the admit until date on your Form I -94, your visa is no longer valid pursuant to Section 222(g) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (INA). You may be required to apply for a new nonimmigrant visa and obtain a waiver of inadmissibility depending on how long you remained in the United States past the admit until date as shown on your Form I-94.

You may be subject to grounds of inadmissibility outlined in section 212 of the Immigration and Nationality Act (INA) This applies to all nonimmigrant travelers, including VWP travelers, unless granted benefits from USCIS to remain in the United States.

-   A bar from traveling to the United States may apply if: You remained unlawfully in the United States for a period of one (1) to 180 days. This may not result in being barred from the United States but will require you to apply for a new nonimmigrant visa with your nearest U.S. Embassy or Consulate before future travels to the United States.
-   You remained unlawfully in the United States for a period of 181 days and less than one-year (364 days) pursuant to section 212(a)(9)(B)(i)(I) of the INA, you will be barred from traveling to the United States for a period of three (3) years.
-   You remained unlawfully in the United States for a period of 365 days or more pursuant to section 212(a)(9)(B)(i)(II) of the INA, you will be barred from traveling to the United States for a period of 10 years.

### What is Section 222(g) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (INA)?

Section 222 is a section of the United States Immigration and Nationality Act (INA) which governs visa issuance. Section 222(g) provides for automatic nullification of a visa when a person stays beyond their authorized period of admission. See U.S. code for further information. [https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-faqTitle8-section1202&num=0&edition=prelim](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title8-section1202&num=0&edition=prelim).

### How can I file to extend my stay while in the United States?

Extensions are processed by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Service using Form I-539 (Application to Extend/Change Nonimmigrant Status). For additional information, please visit USCIS at: [www.uscis.gov](https://www.uscis.gov/).

Note: If you were admitted to the United States under the Visa Waiver Program (VWP), you cannot extend your stay.

### As a migrant worker, where can I find information about my rights and worker protections?

Please visit [migrantworker.gov](https://www.dol.gov/general/migrantworker) to learn about your rights and resources that is available to you as a migrant worker. It is against the law for your employer to fire you, refuse to pay you, or take other negative actions against you for exercising your rights.

### As a U.S. employer, or a local, state, or federal agency granting benefits, how do I verify an I-94?

If you are a U.S. employer, or a local, state, or federal agency granting benefits, it is important to confirm that the information on any Form I-94 printout you receive from your employee is accurate. U.S. employers can use the following methods to verify an individual’s immigration status and eligibility to work:

1.  **Form I-9, Employment Eligibility Verification:** Employers must complete Form I-9 for all new hires, verifying identity and employment authorization using acceptable documents (such as a U.S. passport, Permanent Resident Card, Employment Authorization Document, or other approved documents).
2.  **E-Verify:**E-Verify is an online system operated by DHS and SSA that allows employers to electronically confirm the employment eligibility of new hires by comparing information from Form I-9 to government records.
3.  **Review of Acceptable Documents:**Employers must physically examine original documents presented by the employee, such as:
    -   U.S. Passport or Passport Card
    -   Permanent Resident Card (Form I-551)
    -   Employment Authorization Document (Form I-766)
    -   Foreign passport with a valid I-94 with the correct employment-based class of admission
    -   Other documents listed on the Form I-9 Lists of Acceptable Documents

If you are a non-U.S. employer or a non-U.S. federal agency, including any parties other than the individual traveler, you are NOT authorized to retrieve Form I-94 information from this website. To verify the status of someone who has traveled to the United States, please contact CBP through the appropriate channels. CBP personnel may be available at a U.S. Embassy or Consulate in your country of residence, employment, or government.

### If I am a non-immigrant traveler with a valid I-94, do I need to purchase a new I-94 when returning to the U.S. after a short trip abroad?

Under the automatic visa revalidation provision of U.S. immigration law, certain nonimmigrant travelers who briefly visit Canada, Mexico, or (for F and J visa holders) adjacent islands for 30 days or less may re-enter the United States without purchasing a new I-94, provided they have a valid admission stamp or paper Form I-94 endorsed by the Department of Homeland Security (DHS). Automatic revalidation applies only if you do not apply for a new visa while abroad and you retain your original I-94. This provision is described in 8 CFR 214.1(b) and 22 CFR 41.112(d).

If you travel outside these areas, stay longer than 30 days, or surrender your I-94, you will need to obtain and pay for a new I-94 upon re-entry. Always check with U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) for guidance specific to your situation.

### Do I need to apply for a Form I-94 when visiting the U.S. by land border or selected ferry?

A Form I-94 is needed by all visitors except: U.S. Citizens, returning resident aliens, aliens with immigrant visas, and most Canadian citizens visiting or in transit. Travelers will be issued an I-94 during the admission process at the port of entry. If you are traveling via a land border or by ferry from selected ports, you may apply for a provisional I-94 in advance here, saving time while at the port of entry later. If you are a crew member in need of an I-95, one will be issued at the time of arrival.

### Why has the Provisional I-94 fee increased?

The $6 fee for Form I-94 has increased by $24 as mandated by the “One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), enacted in July 2025. Accordingly, the new fee to apply for a CBP Form I-94 at a land border port of entry will be $30.00, consisting of the $6 land border fee and the HR-1 $24 fee. ### When do I need to apply for a Provisional I-94 online?

If you are entering on the United States at a land border or at a selected ferry port within the next seven days, you can apply and pay for your Provisional I-94 online using this website or by using the CBP Link Mobile Application. Travelers entering the United States by air or sea are not eligible to apply for an I-94 from this website. Your I-94 fee is built into to your airline or sea ticket.

Please be aware that the I-94 you obtain online is provisional and just the first step in completing the I-94 application. To obtain the final, legal-for-travel Form I-94, you will need to appear at a port of entry within seven (7) days from the date you initially applied to complete the final steps in person with a CBP officer.

### If I apply and pay for Provisional I-94 online, do I have to appear at the port of entry?

**Yes.** You still need to appear at the port of entry to complete the process and receive an electronic Form I-94. You will only receive a Provisional I-94 from the online application. Paying online can help expedite processing at the port of entry, but it does not guarantee admission into the United States. The final determination is made by the CBP officer during the admission process.

### What happens if I do not appear at a port of entry within seven days?

If you do not appear at a land border port of entry within seven (7) days, your Provisional I-94 will expire, and you will be required to reapply and pay the fee again. **There are no refunds** if the I-94 is not issued within the seven-day period or if you are refused admission.

### If I apply for a Provisional I-94 online, can I be denied entry into the United States?

### After finishing the online application for a Provisional I-94, what do I need to bring to the port of entry to complete the I-94 application process?

To complete your Provisional I-94 application, you will need to do the following:

1.  Appear at a port of entry within seven (7) days. If you do not appear within seven (7) days, the Provisional I-94 will expire, and you will be required to reapply and pay the fee again. There are no refunds.
2.  If applicable, provide your biometrics (fingerprint or facial scan) at the port of entry to complete the interview with the CBP officer.
3.  You may be asked to show evidence of your residence, employment and/or travel plans; therefore, it is a good idea to have this documentation available.
4.  Once your admissibility is determined, the CBP officer will issue you Form I-94 that is retrievable from this website “Get Most Recent I-94/I-95”. If you are not admissible, you will not be issued Form I-94 and be returned to the country of your departure.
5.  Please print and keep a copy of your Form I-94 and provide to any government official, law enforcement officer, organization, institution, or employer that requests evidence of lawful admission. (Note: You can print as many copies of your Form I-94 as necessary)

### Why am I receiving the message “Active Provisional I-94 on File” or “Duplicate” when I try to submit a Provisional I-94 application?

If you receive the message “Active Provisional I-94 on File” then you:

-   May already have the same Passport or Border Crossing Card that has a current unexpired Provisional I-94 issued within seven (7) days,

Once an application is approved and paid, Provisional I-94 remains active for seven (7) days or until the official I-94 is issued by a CBP officer at the land or select ferry port of entry. Unused Provisional I-94s expire in seven (7) days and would then require a new application and fee if you still intend on traveling to the United States.

If you receive the message “Duplicate” then there are two or more Provisional I-94 applications in the same submission that match on the same Passport or Border Crossing Card.

When “duplicates” are identified, the first Provisional I-94 application is kept, and the remaining matching Provisional I-94 applications are removed because there can only be one active Provisional I-94.

### What happens if I accidentally submit the same set of Provisional I-94 applications twice for payment?

The Apply for Provisional I-94 application will not allow duplicate submissions if an unexpired Provisional I-94 already exists in the system. When applying, the process performs validation checks on each application, including verifying whether a Provisional I-94 is currently active. Only applications without errors will be issued.

Note: Please ensure you only submit applications for travelers who require a Provisional I-94. Even if an application is submitted in error, the fee is non-refundable.

### What if I use a different document to enter the United States than the one used when I applied for my Provisional I-94?

If you present a different passport or travel document at the port of entry than the one used for your Provisional I-94 application, you will be required to pay for a new I-94 upon admission. The CBP officer will not be able to locate your Provisional I-94 if the document does not match your application. A new payment is required, and there are no refunds.

### What if I made a biographical error on my Provisional I-94 and the Provisional I-94 does not match the biographical data on my document?

You will have to pay for a new I-94 upon your admission to the United States if the document you present to the CBP officer does not match biographical data on the Provisional I-94. The CBP officer will be unable to locate your Provisional I-94 if your biographical data is not a match. A new payment will be required and there are no refunds.

### What are the Compacts of Free Association (COFA)?

The Compact of Free Association (COFA) Act of 1985 is an international agreement between the United States, the Federated States of Micronesia, the Republic of the Marshall Islands, and the Republic of Palau granting citizens of these nations the eligibility to live and legally work in the United States indefinitely without a visa. These three countries are sometimes referred to collectively as the “Freely Associated States.” You can find additional information on COFA at [https://www.uscis.gov/i-9-central/complete-correct-form-i-9/federated-states-of-micronesia-republic-of-the-marshall-islands-and-palau](https://www.uscis.gov/i-9-central/completing-form-i-9/federated-states-of-micronesia-republic-of-the-marshall-islands-and-palau "Federated States of Micronesia, Republic of the Marshall Islands, and Palau")

### What do I do if I am unable to retrieve my most recent I-94 or travel history?

If you are unable to retrieve your most recent I-94 or travel history, when querying the I-94/I-95 website, please be sure you are entering your first name, last name, date of birth, document number, and citizenship exactly as they appear on your passport that you used when entering the United States. The screenshot below illustrates the information you need when searching for your I-94.

Alternatively, if you have a smart phone (Apple or Android) you can download the CBP Link Mobile Application and create a free account with Login.gov and once you sign into CBP Link you can search for your latest I-94 by clicking on the “Get Most Recent I-94/I-95” tab. You will have two ways you can retrieve your I-94 from CBP Link Mobile Application:

-   “Document Scan” feature - Take a photo of the Machine-Readable Zone (MRZ) section of your passport.
-   Manually enter your biographical data into the application

If you are still unable to retrieve your most recent I-94 or travel history or are unsure if you were issued an I-94, you can submit a request to U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) Information Center (CIC) at [U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) Information Center (CIC)](https://www.help.cbp.gov/s/?language=en_US "U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) Information Center (CIC)") Under “Ask a Question” select “Topic” and the “I-94 / Traveler Compliance” then under “Applicable Issue” select “CHNV”. Please provide a clear and detailed account of the issue you're experiencing when attempting to retrieve your latest I-94 record in the “Description” section. You must also submit a legible copy of your current passport.

If you are an attorney or a third-party requesting information on behalf of someone else (other than the applicant's minor child) you must also attach a written consent from the first party whose record is being requested in addition to a copy of the traveler's current passport. It is recommended that you use **Form** [G-28](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/forms/g-28.pdf "Form G-28") and that is signed by you and the first party whose record is being retrieved.

If you have further questions, you may visit the [CBP Information Center](https://www.help.cbp.gov/s/?language=en_US "CBP Information Center") or contact 1-877-227-5511 (within the United States) or 00+1+202-325-8000 (Outside of the United States) for assistance.

### What is the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA)?

The Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) is a federal statute. FOIA generally provides that any person has a right to request access to federal agency records. FOIA also establishes a presumption that records in the possession of agencies and departments of the Executive Branch of the U. S. government are accessible to the people, except to the extent the records are protected from disclosure by any of nine exemptions contained in the law or by one of three special law enforcement record exclusions.

### What is a FOIA request?

A FOIA request is a written request received by a U.S. federal agency from any individual or entity requesting records including databases held or believed to be held by an agency.

### How do I submit a FOIA Request?

You can submit a FOIA request by providing a clear and detailed description of the records you are seeking. Including specific information such as dates, times, officer names (if available), certificate numbers, document types, entry numbers, and other relevant details will help expedite the search process and increase the likelihood of receiving the information you need.

**Note:** Travel records are only available from 1982 onward. FOIA does not require agencies to answer questions; requests must be for records, not for information or answers to questions. FOIA requests submitted as questions will not be processed.

If requesting information about yourself, please include as much information as possible to assist us in locating the record(s) you are seeking, to include your Date of Birth, Alien number (A number), your parents’ names, and any alias you may have used at the time of entry or apprehension. It is recommended that you submit a signed Certification of Identify form, or a perjury statement and signature or notary.

If you are an attorney or a third-party requesting information on behalf of someone else (other than your own minor child) you must attach written consent from the subject of record. It is recommended that you use Form [G-28](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/forms/g-28.pdf "Form G-28")

### Does the G-28 FOIA form need to be signed by an accredited individual or notarized?

DHS regulations require, in the case of third-party information requests, a statement from the individual verifying his or her identity and certifying that individual's agreement that records concerning them may be accessed, analyzed, and released to a third party. It is recommended that you submit a signed Certification of Identify form, or a perjury statement and signature or notary.

### How can I use the I-94/I-95 website as a crewmember with an I-95 landing permit?

The I-94/I-95 website provides two sections relevant to crewmembers with an I-95 landing permit:

1.  Get Most Recent I-94/I-95: This section allows you to print your most recent Form I-95, which can be used as evidence of lawful admission or landing.

2.  View Travel History: This section displays your United States arrival and departure dates for the past ten years.

The “Apply for I-94” feature is not available to crewmembers. Form I-95 is only issued in person by a CBP officer at an airport or seaport of entry.

The “View Compliance” section is not available to crewmembers holding Form I-95. This feature provides compliance information only to nonimmigrant travelers admitted under the following classifications:

-   Visa Waiver Program (VWP)
-   Guam-Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands Visa Waiver Program (G-CNMI)
-   CNMI Economic Vitality & Security Travel Authorization Program (EVS-TAP)

### What information must I provide in order to obtain my most recent I-95 or travel history?

To retrieve your most recent Form I-95 or your travel history, use the Document Capture feature or manually enter the required information to conduct a search. Enter the information exactly as it appears on the travel document you used to enter the United States, which is typically your passport.

#### Sample Passport

-   1
-   2
-   3
-   4
-   5
-   6
-   7

The name format on a passport can be ambiguous, the data in the Machine-Readable Zone (MRZ) at the base should be used instead. This data follows the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) standard convention.

1.  **Passport Country of Issuance** - Select your passport country of issuance corresponding to the country code appearing in zone 1 on the MRZ of your passport. The country of citizenship on your passport is the passport issuing authority regardless of the country from which the passport was physically obtained.

3.  **Passport Number** - Enter your passport number from the MRZ of your passport as shown above in zone 2. The passport number may contain numbers and/or letters. Please closely distinguish between the two. Some commonly confused characters are the number zero (0) and letter 'O', and the number one (1) and letter 'I'. When seeking travel history, you must enter the passport number from which you wish to obtain the history. If you do not have the original passport number used for your entry, you will need to submit a Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) request to obtain your most recent I-94/I-95 and travel information. For more information about FOIA requests, refer to the FAQs above.

5.  **Last/Surname and First (Given) Name** - Enter your family name and your first (given) name as shown above in zones 3 and 4 from the MRZ of your passport. Note, the system WILL NOT accept special characters such as a tilde (~) or accent marks (\`). Use only the alphabetic characters shown in the MRZ. If there are any chevrons (<) in the last name zone, enter spaces in place of the chevrons.

7.  **Date of Birth** - Enter the day, month, and year you were born, which should match the date of birth in the upper portion of your passport.

### How do I print my I-95 form?

Travelers can print their own Form I-95 from this website using the "Get Most Recent I-94/I-95" query. Once the traveler retrieves their I-95 there is a "print" button at the bottom of the results page which will give you the option to print the information displayed.

### What do I do if I am unable to retrieve information about my most recent I-95 or travel history from the website?

If you are unable to retrieve your most recent I-95 or travel history on the CBP I-94/I-95 Website, please try the following:

-   Make sure you're entering your name, date of birth, document number, and country of citizenship as they appear in the travel document used when you entered the United States. (Note: if more than one last or first name is listed in your passport, please be sure you are entering both names as they appear in your passport.)

If you are still unable to retrieve your I-95, you can file a redress with the CBP Information Center at [Ask a Question](https://www.help.cbp.gov/s/questions?language=en_US "Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) request") and choose the Topic “I-94/Traveler Compliance” and choose applicable issue “Issue with my I-94/Travel History”. Once you complete your email address and description of your issue, you can upload support documents and click “Next” so you can submit your redress for help.

If you have further questions, you may visit the [CBP Information Center](https://www.help.cbp.gov/s/?language=en_US) or contact 1-877-227-5511 (within the United States) or 00+1+202-325-8000 (Outside of the United States) for assistance.

### Can an I-95 be used for multiple entries?

While the paper Form I-95 can be reused for multiple entries, the electronic version is single entry only. For each arrival as a crew member and landing request, a new electronic Form I-95 will be issued for each entry.

### How long will my crew landing record or Form I-95 be available on the I-94/I-95 website?

Crewmembers can access their most recent landing record (Form I-95) on the I-94/I-95 website, if it was created. "Most recent" means the website will only display the latest Form I-95 that matches the provided name and date of birth.

Please note: The availability of the Form I-95 record does not grant legal authority to remain in the United States beyond the terms and conditions of your admission.

### What is the departure feature on the CBP Link Mobile Application?

The departure feature permits a non-immigrant alien to record their departure from the United States where their departure was not captured at the time of departure. This is especially true when departing by land or sea, but can be used for commercial air travels, but the air carrier is responsible for registering your departure.

The requirements for using the CBP Link Mobile Application are:

-   After you physically exit the United States, open the CBP Link app, select “Departing Traveler,” then select “Verify Departure.”
-   You must be at least three (3) miles outside of the United States border
-   Turn on the “Use my Location” feature on your smartphone
-   Take a selfie on the CBP Link Mobile Application
-   Enter your biographical information (name, date of birth, sex, passport or Alien number, and the country you are currently in before submitting on the CBP Home Mobile Application (i.e. _Departed from the U.S. State of Arizona into Mexico by walking, you will enter “Mexico” as the country you are currently in_)
