What information can I obtain from the I-94/I-95 website?
The I-94/I-95 website consists of four main sections:

Apply for a Provisional Form I-94: Enables users to apply for a Provisional I-94 up to seven days before arriving in the United States at a port of entry or select ferry locations. For more details, refer to the "Provisional I-94s" section of the FAQs.

Get Most Recent I-94/I-95: Allows retrieval and printing of most recent I-94 admission record for most classes of admission or parole.

View Travel History: Provides access to the past 10 years of travel history for certain foreign nationals.

View Compliance: Displays the remaining authorized time in the United States and overstay status for specific classes of admission, including:

Visa Waiver Program (VWP)
Guam-Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands Visa Waiver Program (G-CNMI VWP)
CNMI Economic Vitality & Security Travel Authorization Program (EVS-TAP)

What is the difference between the Form I-95 and I-94?
The Form I-95 is the Crewman’s Landing Permit which is issued only to working alien crewmembers on a vessel or aircraft. The I-95 gives the working alien crewmember permission to enter the United States for up to 29 days or depart with their vessel or aircraft. Whichever comes first.

The Form I-94 is the Arrival/Departure Form that is issued to only non-immigrant aliens entering the United States for tourism, business, employment, or student status. The I-94 allows the alien non-immigrant to remain in the United States until the Admit Until Date annotated on their Form I-94 by the admitting CBP officer.

I am a United States Citizen or Lawful Permanent Resident. Can I retrieve my travel history by using the I-94/I-95 website?
No, United States Citizens and Lawful Permanent Residents cannot retrieve their travel history using the I-94/I-95 website. Currently, only nonimmigrant can use this feature. To obtain your travel history records, you will need to submit a request through the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA). Please refer to the "Freedom of Information Act (FOIA)" section of the FAQs for instructions on how to submit your request.

Will CBP provide a traveler with documentation or evidence showing their status and authorized time in the United States?

Does the I-94 automation process help eliminate errors, and what should a traveler do if their Form I-94 or admission is incorrect?
Yes, the electronic I-94 helps reduce errors by using information from official travel document. However, if there is an error on either the paper or electronic Form I-94 issued by CBP, or if a nonimmigrant traveler was admitted incorrectly to the United States, they should contact their nearest Deferred Inspection office within thirty days of admission or parole, or you may submit an inquiry through the CBP Ask a Question portal at https://www.help.cbp.gov/s/questions?language=en_US. A list of Deferred Inspection Sites and Ports of Entry is available on the CBP website (https://www.cbp.gov) under the "Ports" link.

If your Form I-94 that was issued by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Service (USCIS), please contact USCIS for assistance with the correction or replacement of your Form I-94 at www.uscis.gov.

How can a traveler revalidate their electronic Form I-94?
If your I-94 admission record is electronic, your I-94 is maintained in CBP systems and can be verified by a CBP officer for automatic visa revalidation, provided you meet the eligibility requirements. To demonstrate eligibility when boarding a carrier to the United States, you may need to print a copy of your electronic I-94 from the CBP website and present it to the air or sea carrier before boarding.

If you were admitted to the United States and received a paper Form I-94, you must present the valid, unexpired paper I-94 and required travel documents to meet automatic revalidation requirements.

For more information about automatic revalidation, please visit the U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) Information Center Automatic revalidation for certain temporary visitors.

How does CBP's automation of Form I-94 affect the I-9 process?
Form I-9, administered by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS), is used to verify the identity and employment authorization of individuals hired in the United States. The automation of Form I-94 does not impact the Form I-9 process. However, the public may notice that I-94 forms now appear differently, some may have original stamps and handwriting, while others may be printouts without any original writing or stamps. Nonimmigrant travelers can print their Form I-94 from the CBP website using the "Get Most Recent I-94/I-95" function or through the CBP Link Mobile Application. Both versions are acceptable for Form I-9 purposes. For more information about Form I-9, visit https://www.uscis.gov/i-9.

What should I do if I am unable to retrieve information about my most recent I-94 or travel history on the CBP I-94/I-95 website?
If you are unable to retrieve your most recent I-94 or travel history, please try the following steps:

Utilize the Document Capture feature on the site. Selecting the "CAPTURE DOCUMENT" button allows you to automatically populate applicant data by utilizing your device’s camera to capture an image of the applicant’s document or by uploading a file containing an image of the document’s biographic page.
Ensure you are entering your name, date of birth, document number, and country of citizenship exactly as they appear in the travel document you used to enter the United States. If your passport lists multiple first or last names, enter all names as shown in your passport.
If you are using the CBP Link Mobile Application and have signed in with Login.gov, select the "Get Most Recent I-94/I-95" tab to search for your latest I-94. You can also use the “document scan” function by taking a photo of the Machine-Readable Zone (MRZ) section of your passport.
If you still cannot retrieve your information, you can file a redress request with the CBP Information Center. Goto “Ask Us a Question” choose the topic “I-94/Traveler Compliance,” and select the issue “Issue with my I-94/Travel History.” Enter your email address, describe your issue, upload supporting documents, and click “Next” to submit your request for assistance.

Can a Form I-94 be used for multiple land or sea entries?
A valid, unexpired Form I-94 issued under most classes of admission may permit multiple entries to the United States by land or sea from contiguous territories (Canada, Mexico, or Adjacent Islands (excluding Cuba)). The traveler may remain in the United States until the "Admit Until Date" specified on their I-94.

Note: Short trips (less than 30 days) by land or ferry to contiguous territories will not result in a new Form I-94 or extend your current "Admit Until Date."

For assistance with reentry to the United States after visiting a contiguous territory, please contact the CBP Information Center at 1-877-227-5511 (inside the United States) or 00+1+202-325-8000 (outside the United States).

What does my I-94 number look like?
I-94 numbers issued before May 2019 are 11 digits long and consist only of numbers (e.g., 01234567890).

Starting in May 2019, CBP began issuing alphanumeric I-94 numbers. These numbers are also 11 characters long and follow the format of nine numbers, one letter, and one final number (e.g., 012345678A1). Unexpired I-94s issued in the numeric-only format remain valid until the "Admit Until Date" shown on the paper I-94 or in the "Get Most Recent I-94/I-95" section of the website, unless otherwise terminated.

How long will my admission record and Form I-94 be available for retrieval on the I-94/I-95 website?
Most travelers can access their most recent admission record (Form I-94) on the I-94/I-95 website if it was issued after 1983. The website will only display the most recently created I-94 that matches the provided name and date of birth. For example, if a traveler entered the United States in 2005 and received a Form I-94, then returned in 2017 and was issued a new Form I-94, only the 2017 record will be shown. Earlier I-94 records cannot be retrieved.

Travelers admitted for the duration of status (D/S) based on their class of admission can access their Form I-94 information regardless of the creation date, as long as an electronic record exists. Examples of D/S classes of admission include A1, A2, C2, F1, F2, FSM, G1, G2, G3, G4, I, J1, J2, MIS, N1, N2, N3, N4,N5, N6, PAL, and T6.

Note: The availability of your I-94 record does not grant legal authority to remain in the United States beyond the terms and conditions of your admission. All travelers must comply with the requirements of their visa classification and the admit until date listed on their I-94.

Where does the travel history on this site come from?
CBP collects travel information from various sources. The travel history available on this website is intended as a helpful tool for the public and is not an official record for legal purposes. Certain types of travel may not appear on the website, including most land border arrivals and departures, closed loop cruise arrivals and departures, air carrier reservation updates, and I-94s issued by USCIS for changes of status, extensions of stay, or adjustments of status.

Travel history for U.S. citizens and lawful permanent residents is not available on this website. To obtain your travel history, please submit a Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) request at www.cbp.gov/FOIA.

What steps should I do if I receive the “10 Days Remaining” reminder email from CBP?
If you received an email indicating that you have 10 days left on your admission and you have already departed, please go to “View Travel History” to confirm your departure is appearing. If you see your departure listed, there is no action to take. The email is automatically generated based on the admission date you received upon entry and serves as a reminder about the time you have left in the United States. The email may have been generated at or near the time you departed the United States.

If your departure is not listed on your travel history, then your departure may not have been properly registered. Some of the most common reasons (not all inclusive) why this may happen is due to:

You used a different citizenship (passport) for departure than you used for arrival.
You used a slightly different variation of your name on departure than you used on arrival.
Your airline misspelled your name or did not list you on the manifest.
You departed by land into Mexico.
You departed on a transatlantic or open loop cruise, and your cruise line mistakenly indicated your cruise was a closed loop cruise.
To request assistance; please file a redress with the CBP Information Center at: Ask a Question. Please choose the appropriate “Topic”: I-94/Traveler Compliance and “Applicable Issue”: 10-Day Email Notification. You must submit supporting evidence that you departed timely in order to review your request for correction.

If you are still in the United States and have not departed, you must leave by 11:59 PM Eastern Time (ET) on the last day of your admission, as indicated by the admit until date on your I-94.

If I have received a 10-Day Email Notification and/or the Traveler Compliance Check shows that I have overstayed my admission; is this a legally binding determination?
No. The Traveler Compliance Check is a tool to assist travelers in determining if they have violated their terms of admission. It is not a legally binding determination of your inadmissibility.

A CBP officer will make the determination on your admissibility at the time you present yourself at a port of entry and ask for admission to the United States. Likewise, the U.S. Department of State has the final determination on whether to issue you a new or renewal nonimmigrant visa.

What do I do if I am unable to leave the United States by the last day of my admission?
If you were admitted to the United States under the Visa Waiver Program (VWP), you cannot extend your stay. In very limited circumstances, a local USCIS director or a CBP officer at your nearest Deferred Inspection site may grant a 30-day “Satisfactory Departure” period on a case-by-case basis if you entered as a Visa Waiver traveler. You must demonstrate that extreme or emergency circumstances are preventing your departure from the United States. For more information, refer to USCIS Chapter 2 – Emergencies or Unforeseen Circumstances-Related Flexibilities, or visit a CBP Deferred Inspection Site at https://www.cbp.gov/contact/ports/deferred-inspection-sites.

If your current class of admission allows extensions, changes, or adjustments and you wish to file a petition to remain past your admit until date on your Form I-94, please contact U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Service (USCIS) for additional assistance or visit USCIS at: www.uscis.gov.

What happens if I overstay my admission period?
If you entered the United States under the Visa Waiver Program and stayed longer than the admit until date on your Form I-94, you are no longer eligible for the Electronic System Travel Authorization (ESTA) under Visa Waiver Program and if your ESTA is still valid, it will be denied. You will require a nonimmigrant visa for all future travels to the United States. For information about applying for a U.S. nonimmigrant visa, please visit the U.S. Department of State by visiting https://www.state.gov.

If you entered the United States on a nonimmigrant visa, such as a tourist visa (B1/B2) and you stayed longer than the admit until date on your Form I -94, your visa is no longer valid pursuant to Section 222(g) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (INA). You may be required to apply for a new nonimmigrant visa and obtain a waiver of inadmissibility depending on how long you remained in the United States past the admit until date as shown on your Form I-94.

You may be subject to grounds of inadmissibility outlined in section 212 of the Immigration and Nationality Act (INA) This applies to all nonimmigrant travelers, including VWP travelers, unless granted benefits from USCIS to remain in the United States.

A bar from traveling to the United States may apply if: You remained unlawfully in the United States for a period of one (1) to 180 days. This may not result in being barred from the United States but will require you to apply for a new nonimmigrant visa with your nearest U.S. Embassy or Consulate before future travels to the United States.
You remained unlawfully in the United States for a period of 181 days and less than one-year (364 days) pursuant to section 212(a)(9)(B)(i)(I) of the INA, you will be barred from traveling to the United States for a period of three (3) years.
You remained unlawfully in the United States for a period of 365 days or more pursuant to section 212(a)(9)(B)(i)(II) of the INA, you will be barred from traveling to the United States for a period of 10 years.

As a U.S. employer, or a local, state, or federal agency granting benefits, how do I verify an I-94?
If you are a U.S. employer, or a local, state, or federal agency granting benefits, it is important to confirm that the information on any Form I-94 printout you receive from your employee is accurate. U.S. employers can use the following methods to verify an individual’s immigration status and eligibility to work:

Form I-9, Employment Eligibility Verification:
Employers must complete Form I-9 for all new hires, verifying identity and employment authorization using acceptable documents (such as a U.S. passport, Permanent Resident Card, Employment Authorization Document, or other approved documents).
E-Verify:
E-Verify is an online system operated by DHS and SSA that allows employers to electronically confirm the employment eligibility of new hires by comparing information from Form I-9 to government records.
Review of Acceptable Documents:
Employers must physically examine original documents presented by the employee, such as:
U.S. Passport or Passport Card
Permanent Resident Card (Form I-551)
Employment Authorization Document (Form I-766)
Foreign passport with a valid I-94 with the correct employment-based class of admission
Other documents listed on the Form I-9 Lists of Acceptable Documents
If you are a non-U.S. employer or a non-U.S. federal agency, including any parties other than the individual traveler, you are NOT authorized to retrieve Form I-94 information from this website. To verify the status of someone who has traveled to the United States, please contact CBP through the appropriate channels. CBP personnel may be available at a U.S. Embassy or Consulate in your country of residence, employment, or government.

If I am a non-immigrant traveler with a valid I-94, do I need to purchase a new I-94 when returning to the U.S. after a short trip abroad?
Under the automatic visa revalidation provision of U.S. immigration law, certain nonimmigrant travelers who briefly visit Canada, Mexico, or (for F and J visa holders) adjacent islands for 30 days or less may re-enter the United States without purchasing a new I-94, provided they have a valid admission stamp or paper Form I-94 endorsed by the Department of Homeland Security (DHS). Automatic revalidation applies only if you do not apply for a new visa while abroad and you retain your original I-94. This provision is described in 8 CFR 214.1(b) and 22 CFR 41.112(d).

If you travel outside these areas, stay longer than 30 days, or surrender your I-94, you will need to obtain and pay for a new I-94 upon re-entry. Always check with U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) for guidance specific to your situation.

Do I need to apply for a Form I-94 when visiting the U.S. by land border or selected ferry?
A Form I-94 is needed by all visitors except: U.S. Citizens, returning resident aliens, aliens with immigrant visas, and most Canadian citizens visiting or in transit. Travelers will be issued an I-94 during the admission process at the port of entry. If you are traveling via a land border or by ferry from selected ports, you may apply for a provisional I-94 in advance here, saving time while at the port of entry later. If you are a crew member in need of an I-95, one will be issued at the time of arrival.
